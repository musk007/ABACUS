**Cohort morphology (n = 127 lymphoblasts):** predominantly medium cells (78.7%) with abundant (83.5%), slightly basophilic (100.0%) cytoplasm and absent cytoplasmic vacuolation (100.0%); nuclei show coarse chromatin (90.5%), regular contours (61.4%), and inconspicuous nucleoli (74.0%).

**Morphologic interpretation:** The cohort shows a predominantly medium-sized, cytoplasm-rich pattern, with strongly dominant coarse nuclear chromatin and predominantly inconspicuous nucleoli producing a relatively condensed nuclear appearance. Abundant cytoplasm with uniformly slight basophilia and absent vacuolation argues against a deeply basophilic, vacuolated morphologic pattern. Nuclear contours are variable, with regular contours only weakly dominant, so nuclear shape is less consistent than chromatin texture or cytoplasmic staining.

**Diagnostic flags:** blasts present; blast threshold met.

**Impression:** Findings consistent with an acute leukaemia of lymphoblastic morphology. Definitive classification requires clinical correlation and ancillary studies.

Lymphoblasts comprise 92.7% of WBCs (blast-equivalent burden of 92.7%), exceeding the 20% blast threshold for acute leukaemia.

**Differential considerations:**
- B- or T-lymphoblastic leukaemia/lymphoma.
- Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (excluded by immunophenotyping).

**Recommended workup:**
- Flow cytometric immunophenotyping for lineage assignment (B-ALL: CD19, CD22, CD79a, CD10, TdT; T-ALL: cytoCD3, CD7, TdT).
- Bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy.
- Cytogenetics and FISH for recurrent ALL abnormalities (BCR::ABL1, KMT2A rearrangements, ETV6::RUNX1, TCF3::PBX1, hypodiploidy).
- Molecular studies including BCR::ABL1-like signature where available.
- CSF examination to assess CNS involvement.
