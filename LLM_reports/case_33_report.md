**Cohort morphology (n = 18 myeloblasts):** predominantly medium cells (55.6%) with abundant (94.4%), moderately basophilic (100.0%) cytoplasm and absent cytoplasmic vacuolation (100.0%); nuclei show open chromatin (100.0%), irregular contours (100.0%), and prominent nucleoli (100.0%).

**Morphologic interpretation:** The myeloblast cohort shows uniformly open nuclear chromatin, prominent nucleoli, and irregular nuclear contours, supporting a consistent pattern of nuclear immaturity. Nearly universal abundant cytoplasm with uniform moderate basophilia demonstrates that this immature nuclear pattern is not accompanied by cytoplasmic paucity. Cell size is variable, with medium size only weakly dominant, contrasting with the uniform nuclear features. Cytoplasmic vacuolation is uniformly absent.

**Diagnostic flags:** blasts present; blast threshold met.

**Impression:** Findings consistent with an acute leukaemia of myeloblastic morphology. Definitive classification requires clinical correlation and ancillary studies.

Myeloid blast-like burden is 46.1% of informative WBCs (overall blast-equivalent burden 46.1%), supporting AML even when mature myeloid cells are also prominent.

**Differential considerations:**
- AML with recurrent genetic abnormalities (per WHO/ICC).
- Acute myeloid leukaemia.
- Mixed-phenotype acute leukaemia (requires immunophenotyping).

**Recommended workup:**
- Flow cytometric immunophenotyping (myeloid and monocytic markers).
- Bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy.
- Cytogenetics and FISH for recurrent AML abnormalities.
- Molecular panel including NPM1, FLT3, CEBPA, RUNX1, and TP53.

**Not assessed:** erythrocyte and platelet morphology, dysplastic features, smear staining quality, and whether fields originate from the optimal monolayer zone. Findings are based on leukocyte morphology only.
