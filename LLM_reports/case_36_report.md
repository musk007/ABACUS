**Cohort morphology (n = 187 promonocytes):** predominantly medium cells (62.0%) with abundant (100.0%), moderately basophilic (100.0%) cytoplasm and prominent cytoplasmic vacuolation (85.0%); nuclei show open chromatin (100.0%), irregular contours (100.0%), and prominent nucleoli (100.0%).

**Morphologic interpretation:** The promonocyte cohort shows uniformly open nuclear chromatin, prominent nucleoli, and irregular nuclear contours, supporting an immature monocytic morphologic pattern. Abundant, moderately basophilic cytoplasm is uniform, with strongly dominant prominent vacuolation providing a consistent cytoplasmic counterpart to the nuclear immaturity. Cell size is more variable, with medium size only weakly dominant over large cells despite the otherwise highly consistent nuclear and cytoplasmic attributes.

**Diagnostic flags:** blasts present; left shifted myeloid.

**Impression:** Findings consistent with an acute leukaemia showing monocytic differentiation. Definitive classification requires clinical correlation and ancillary studies.

Monocytic-lineage predominance (promonocytes 88.2%) with a blast-equivalent burden of 1.9% is suspicious for a monocytic AML pattern under sparse annotation conditions.

**Differential considerations:**
- Acute monoblastic/monocytic leukaemia.
- Acute myelomonocytic leukaemia.
- Reactive monocytosis with immature forms (less likely).

**Recommended workup:**
- Flow cytometric immunophenotyping (CD64, CD14, CD11b, lysozyme, CD33).
- Bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy.
- Cytogenetics and targeted molecular AML panel.
